I’m a robotics and autonomous systems engineer focused on building reliable, real-world driving intelligence. I’m currently pursuing a Master’s in Electrical and Computer Engineering (Robotics track) at the University of Michigan, where I work at MCity and UMTRI on autonomy, simulation, and planning for self-driving vehicles. My background spans electrical engineering, machine learning, and robotics, with hands-on experience across perception, planning, and control.

My work covers the full autonomy stack. I’ve developed ROS2 C++ localization and trajectory planning modules in Autoware for semi co-driving systems, validated through closed-loop simulation in CARLA and VI-grade. I’ve also built large-scale synthetic data pipelines using Parallel Domain and HD maps to generate multimodal datasets and reduce the sim-to-real gap, alongside hands-on work in stereo vision, multi-camera and LiDAR calibration, and real-time deployment on embedded platforms.

On the research side, I focus on efficiency-aware autonomy and learning-based planning. I work on Vision-Language-Action models for driving, integrating token-efficient techniques like ToMe into modern planners and evaluating them in closed-loop benchmarks such as Bench2Drive and NAVSIM. I also develop transformer-based end-to-end driving planners using large-scale datasets like Waymo, with the long-term goal of designing scalable, safe, and efficient autonomy systems that perform reliably in the real world
