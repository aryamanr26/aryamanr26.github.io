I’m a robotics engineer with a strong passion for perception and planning in field robotics—building systems that can reliably sense their environment and make smart decisions in complex, real-world settings.

My background combines electrical engineering, machine learning, and robotics, with hands-on experience across the full autonomy stack. I’ve developed ROS2-based planning modules in C++ for trajectory control in Autoware, tested them in simulations like CARLA and VI-grade, built synthetic data pipelines with tools like Parallel Domain to generate diverse RGB, LiDAR, and radar scenarios, and handled sensor calibration for multi-camera and LiDAR systems to ensure accurate fusion and localization. 

On the research side, I explore efficient vision-language-action models, token-efficient inference techniques, and transformer-based end-to-end planning, evaluating them on benchmarks like Bench2Drive and NAVSIM to balance performance and safety. Currently, I’m pursuing a Master’s in Electrical and Computer Engineering (Robotics track) at the University of Michigan, contributing to autonomy and simulation projects at MCity and UMTRI
